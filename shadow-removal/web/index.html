Remove shadows from outdoor photos

Upload a photo with a hard shadow on grass, road or pavement. Umbra finds the shadow in LAB color space, relights it to match the sunlit ground around it, and shows you the mask and the result. Processing runs in your browser, so the photo never leaves your device.

How it works

The same pipeline as the Python version of this project, running on a canvas.

01

Convert to LAB

A shadow lowers lightness (L) while chromaticity (a, b) stays close, which separates it from a dark object.

02

Threshold

Pixels below mean minus std / k on L, or on L + a + b for strongly colored scenes, become shadow candidates. The sensitivity slider is k.

03

Clean the mask

Morphological open and close, a YCbCr check, then only regions of meaningful size are kept, so grass blades and gravel are not read as shadow.

04

Relight

Each LAB channel of the shadow is rescaled to the mean and spread of a ring of lit pixels around it, then the seam is blurred.

Where it struggles

  • It assumes one dominant shadow on a fairly uniform surface. Many small shadows, or shadows across several materials, are corrected only partly.
  • Very dark or very soft shadows can leave a faint outline, because relighting matches statistics and does not recover the true pixels.
  • Dark objects that are not shadows, such as a black car, can be marked as shadow. Raise the minimum region size or lower sensitivity if that happens.
  • This browser version blurs the seam. The Python version of the project uses Telea inpainting for that step.
Umbra is a coursework project for Image Processing. Photos are processed locally and are not uploaded.
